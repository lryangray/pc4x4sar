THE TEN ESSENTIAL SYSTEMS — CARRY ALL 10, EVERY TRIP

1      Navigation                     Topo map + compass + GPS/phone with offline maps. Know how to use them all.

2      Headlamp                       LED headlamp + extra batteries. Test before every trip. Hands-free light.

3      Sun Protection                 SPF 30+ sunscreen, UV sunglasses, hat, lip balm with SPF. Reapply every 2 hrs.

4      First Aid Kit                  Bandages, gauze, tape, pain meds, blister care, personal meds. Check dates.

5      Knife + Repair                 Multi-tool, duct tape (3+ ft), cord/paracord (50 ft), zip ties, safety pins.

6      Fire Starting                  Waterproof matches AND lighter + fire starter. Both — redundancy matters.

7      Emergency Shelter              Bivy, space blanket, or ultralight tarp. Practice setup at home first.

8      Extra Food                     1+ extra day's worth. Bars, nuts, jerky, peanut butter. No cooking required.

9      Extra Water                    1L+ extra + purification (filter, tablets, or UV). Pre-hydrate before hike.

10      Extra Clothing                 Insulating layer, rain jacket + pants, warm hat, gloves. NO COTTON.

FIELD TIPS

❑ Total weight: A complete kit weighs only 3-5 lbs. There is no excuse to skip it.

❑ Cotton kills: Loses ALL insulation when wet. Wool or synthetic materials only.

❑ Cold batteries: Lose 20-40% capacity in cold. Keep phone and headlamp in inside pockets.

❑ Pre-hydrate: Drink 16 oz in the hour before you start. Dark urine = already dehydrated.

❑ Keep it packed: Dedicated bag, always ready to grab. Check expiration dates each season.

MOST RESCUES INVOLVE DAY HIKERS
The majority of people we rescue planned to be out for 'just a few hours.' Afternoon weather, a wrong turn, or a minor injury can turn a short hike into an overnight. Always pack
as if you might spend the night out. Pierce County spans sea level to 14,411 ft (Mt. Rainier) — prepare for the highest point on your route.
